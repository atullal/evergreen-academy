Lesson 6: Passwords Without Tears

Keeping track of passwords can be frustrating. You are not alone in feeling overwhelmed by them. It is perfectly normal to forget them. Today, we will learn how to make passwords easier to remember and much safer to use.

What Makes a Strong Password?

A Password is a secret key you type into a computer or phone to prove you are the right person.

Many places ask for numbers and symbols, but length is actually more important. A long password is much harder for a computer program to guess.

  1. Instead of a random jumble of letters, try using a short sentence or phrase.
  2. For example, "BlueHorseRunningFast" is a very strong password. (Note: Please do not use this exact example! Always make up your own unique phrase.)
  3. It is easy for you to remember, but very hard for anyone else to guess.

The Danger of Reusing Passwords

It is tempting to use your one favorite password for every website. This is called Password Reuse, and it is the biggest danger on the internet.

  1. Imagine using the exact same metal key for your front door, your car, and your safe.
  2. If a thief steals that one key, they can open everything.
  3. If a bad website gets broken into, criminals will take your password from there and try it on your email or bank account.
  4. You must use a different, unique password for your important accounts.

The Physical Password Book

You might wonder how you can possibly remember a different password for every website. The answer is simple: you do not have to.

  1. A Password Book is a physical paper notebook where you write down your passwords.
  2. It is completely sensible and safe to write your passwords down on paper.
  3. Criminals on the internet cannot reach through the computer to open your desk drawer.
  4. Keep your notebook at home, in a safe place away from visitors.
  5. When a website asks for your password, just open your book and copy it carefully.

Try it yourself

  1. Find a small notebook you can dedicate to your passwords.
  2. Think of a long, memorable phrase you could use as a new password. (Remember: do not use "CoffeeInTheGarden123" or any example from this lesson. Create your own!)
  3. Write down the name of a website, the email address you use to log in, and your new phrase.
  4. Safety tip: If you are ready to actually change an important password online, ask a family member or someone you trust to sit with you the first time. This makes sure you do not accidentally get locked out of your account!

Printable Summary: Passwords Without Tears

Password: A secret key used to unlock your accounts. Make it long: A long phrase (like "BlueHorseRunningFast") is stronger and easier to remember than a short jumble of symbols. Password Reuse: Using the exact same password everywhere. This is dangerous because if one site is tricked, criminals can try that password on your other accounts. Password Book: A paper notebook for your passwords. It is very safe to write passwords down, as long as the book stays inside your home. * Ask for help: When changing a password online for the first time, have someone you trust sit with you so you do not accidentally lock yourself out.
